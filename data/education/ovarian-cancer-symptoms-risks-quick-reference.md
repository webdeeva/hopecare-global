# Ovarian Cancer: Symptoms & Risks Quick Reference

Awareness is the first step toward early detection. This guide provides a quick look at the most common symptoms and risk factors associated with ovarian cancer.

## Common Symptoms

These indicators are most concerning when they are **new, persistent, and frequent**—occurring often, on most days, over a stretch of weeks.

- 🎈 **Bloating:** Increased abdominal size or persistent bloating.
- 🍽️ **Eating Complications:** Difficulty eating or feeling full quickly.
- 🩹 **Pelvic or Abdominal Pain:** Pain that doesn't let up.
- 🚽 **Urinary Changes:** Urgent or frequent urination.
- 🩸 **Abnormal Vaginal Bleeding:** Especially post-menopause or irregular for your cycle.
- 💩 **Bowel Habits:** Changes such as constipation or irregularity.
- 😴 **Extreme Fatigue:** Unexplained and persistent exhaustion.

*If these symptoms occur more than 12 times a month, consult your healthcare provider.*

## Know Your Risk

Ovarian cancer can affect any woman, but being aware of your risk helps you advocate for your health.

- **Age:** Risk increases as you get older, particularly after menopause.
- **Family History:** Having a mother, sister, or daughter with ovarian, breast, or colorectal cancer.
- **Genetic Mutations:** Carriers of **BRCA1/BRCA2** or Lynch syndrome have a significantly higher risk.
- **Reproductive History:** Never having been pregnant, or having children later in life.
- **Health Conditions:** Including endometriosis and obesity.

---

### Advocate for Yourself
You are the expert on your own body. If symptoms are new and persistent, speak up: *"These symptoms are new, they happen almost every day, and they've lasted more than two weeks. Could this be related to my ovaries?"*
